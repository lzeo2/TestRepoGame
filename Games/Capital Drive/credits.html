Built around real Canberra.

Capital Drive is an original, single-player exploration and action preview set in Canberra's inner south, Civic and airport approaches. The street network, building outlines and terrain come from the datasets below. All gameplay data and rendering dependencies are served locally.

Street network and OpenStreetMap outlines

© OpenStreetMap contributors. The local road and OSM outline database is available under the Open Database License 1.0. The original data was obtained through the official OpenStreetMap map API. The transformed snapshot is available as canberra.json, with source hashes and import details. The independent Civic/lake overlay adds the source road connection across both bridges, mapped Centre buildings and the lake shoreline with island holes. It also retains its pinned OSM API source hashes and ODbL terms. The additional road profile sidecar retains exact cached OSM service/access tags so driveways and parking aisles receive smaller presentation widths and are excluded from ambient public traffic.

Offline address search

© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The local address index contains 3,446 explicitly tagged records from the bounded OSM snapshot. Some points are approximate building centres. Missing house numbers are never invented or interpolated; coverage is incomplete. Search and play need no remote geocoder.

ACT building footprints

© Australian Capital Territory. ACTGOV_BUILDING_FOOTPRINTS, used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. Footprints have been projected, simplified in presentation and supplemented with OSM outlines. The dataset is incomplete and does not establish current building appearance.

North and west road extension

The local OpenStreetMap road overlay extends the driving network toward Dickson and Lyneham in the north and the western lake, Black Mountain and Yarralumla in the west. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Its contour grid uses ACT 2020 LiDAR-derived two-metre contours, © Australian Capital Territory and Aerometrex Limited, CC BY 4.0. The new ground is a coarse contour interpolation. Existing finer terrain retains priority where the grids overlap.

Northern places and Giant Spray

Selected northern footprints, shop locations, Black Mountain Tower and the Big Splash park and public approach use OpenStreetMap geometry. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The archive, closed-park treatment, store names and frontage designs are original game interpretations.

Canberra regional expansion

The local Belconnen, North Canberra and South Canberra source packs retain mapped streets, landmarks and lake boundaries. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Ground uses ACT 2020 LiDAR-derived two-metre contours, © Australian Capital Territory, CC BY 4.0, sampled at about 100 metres. Façade treatments and shoreline water heights are game estimates.

Australian War Memorial

OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The local source snapshot supplies the exterior footprint, public terraces, lawns and paths. Architectural details are original estimates. The playable gallery entrance, exhibition and missions are original game designs. The Manuka bank branch uses a mapped public location and an original playable layout.

Light rail and ANU

The local rail snapshot follows mapped tracks, stops and directional platforms from Alinga Street to Gungahlin Place. The ANU layer uses existing building footprints and mapped public paths. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The tram and shelter models, timetable, campus façades and missions are original game designs.

Macquarie Shops

Original modelled frontage details use observations from ACT City Services exterior photographs. Photographs and mural art are excluded from the game. The shop buildings use retained OpenStreetMap footprints. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0.

Additional building outlines

Microsoft GlobalML Building Footprints, used under the Community Data License Agreement Permissive 2.0. Machine-derived satellite outlines supplement ACT and OSM data after polygon-overlap checks. Heights remain estimates. The original inner-south snapshot has 12,572 source-derived outlines; the Civic overlay adds OSM outlines after duplicate checks.

Streetlight columns

Selected centre locations, heights and arm types were filtered from the ACT Streetlight Column Assets. © Australian Capital Territory, CC BY 4.0. Other streetlights are placed along mapped streets.

Park barbecues and drinking fountains

Locations and recorded equipment attributes © Australian Capital Territory, CC BY 4.0. The game filters and projects the ACTmapi barbecue and drinking fountain inventories. The models interpret public equipment designs. Masonry uses Rob Tuytel’s Red Brick material, CC0, resized for offline play.

Terrain

© Australian Capital Territory, CC BY 4.0. The current 513 × 513 terrain grid is interpolated from ACT's 2020 LiDAR-derived two-metre contours, with approximately 12-metre horizontal cells. It is not the native one-metre survey DEM. Elevations use AHD metres relative to a 550-metre datum. See data notices and numerical validation and limitations. The separate 259 × 157 northern grid uses the same ACT contour source at about 25-metre spacing, blended into the base. These are contour interpolations, not surveyed bridge decks. The lake uses the normal 555.93 m AHD design level; bridge deck profiles are original height estimates. The initial Mapzen terrain has been superseded; its historical attribution is retained.

Street references

The school, Manuka, Kingston and Red Hill streetscapes were compared with Street View samples. Landmark references include Parliament House (March 2015), Old Parliament House (March 2026), Manuka Oval (September 2020) and a partly obscured Kingston approach (March 2026), alongside official architectural facts. Original procedural models reflect observed architectural cues, paving, fencing and canopy forms. No Google imagery or textures are bundled. These remain approximate models, not photogrammetry.

Rendering, vehicles and fonts

Three.js r160, © the three.js authors, MIT licence. Original fictional procedural vehicles reuse this repository's car-arcade model factory. Atkinson Hyperlegible by the Braille Institute, SIL Open Font License. Interface, driving rules and streetscape details are original work for this project.

Landmark models

Original simplified models place Parliament House, Old Parliament House, Manuka Oval, Kingston Powerhouse, Manuka Pool and Canberra Centre at mapped coordinates. Formal forecourts and the oval field keep clear of procedural trees. Roofs, marble frontage, flag mast, stands, floodlights and industrial forms are interpretations of the references, with lightweight skyline models. Manuka Pool uses the exact cached OSM swimming-basin and site polygons, with an enclosed boundary, a passable gate and original heritage-style entrance detail. Wall materials, gate placement, paving and landscaping are approximate interpretations of public heritage and architectural references. No new pool Street View inspection is claimed. No Street View photos or textures are bundled.

Street activities and buses

Twenty Manuka shops, the Civic noodle restaurant and five nearby Civic shops use explicitly tagged OSM retail locations and matched source footprints, with original parody names such as Coals. Frontage positions are estimates derived from the footprints and adjacent public streets. Their fictional till robberies, food, prices and payouts do not describe the real businesses. The priority Lambzhou 1919 room uses original timber, seating and open-kitchen details informed by two official Canberra restaurant interior photographs. A verified Canberra exterior photograph informs the oxblood fascia, cream transom, charcoal pier and black glazing. The two official interior photographs inform the left preparation counter and right dining side. Door positions and room dimensions remain estimates; no photo, logo or mural assets are bundled. Supplies and job markers remain fictional activities. Four playable gyms include the original Kingston marker and three source-backed parody venues. The local OSM street-assets sidecar retains 388 bus-stop facilities, 13 fuel sites and 24 named fitness venues; headings, furniture and gameplay are interpretations. Two original unbranded buses follow fictional ambient circuits on the real public-road graph and dwell at two compatible source stops; route numbers and timetables are not reproduced. Drivers, pedestrians and officers are fictional adults. The inherited car interiors use a left-side seat and wheel despite the left-hand road traffic.

Civic skate park and hire rides

The Civic skate park uses the exact ACT SKPK6 boundary from the ACT Skate Park Assets dataset, © Australian Capital Territory, CC BY 4.0. Original ramps, rail and ledges interpret published feature descriptions; their positions and heights are not surveyed. The matching opening is cut from the source building in both rendering and collision. Lemon is an original fictional hire service with scooters and e-bikes at estimated safe footpath stands beside mapped roads, not a real operator, fleet or pricing scheme.

Rooms and neighbourhoods

House rooms and shop interiors are original plausible layouts, generated only when entered. The Centre is a partial ground-floor interpretation using published Monaro Mall architectural cues; it is not an inspected whole-centre tenant floorplan. These rooms are not surveyed private interiors. The noodle cook, pink-earmuff diner, three campaign protagonists and all dialogue are original fiction. Gardens and backyards derive from building and road relationships, not cadastral boundaries. Park stunt ramps are fictional gameplay equipment with original placement and geometry. Telopea fencing follows the mapped school site with estimated openings at actual footpath crossings.

Canberra Airport

The local airport overlay retains nine pinned official OpenStreetMap source responses under ODbL 1.0. Pialligo and Terminal avenues, terminal footprint, runways, taxiways and aprons use their source geometry. Terminal height, glazing and roof details are original estimates. The eastern terrain uses a local 89 × 122 contour grid sampled at about 50 metres from ACT 2020 LiDAR-derived two-metre contours, © Australian Capital Territory and Aerometrex Limited, CC BY 4.0. It retains AHD heights with an authored smooth seam and runway grades. It is not surveyed pavement or a native DEM. Approach reflector posts are original estimates along mapped public roads; they are decorative equipment, not sourced guardrails. This is an exterior interpretation; terminal rooms and airline services are not reproduced. Two original unbranded jets are fictional game vehicles, placed at estimated stands on sourced aprons.

What is approximate

Building heights, roof forms, facades, vegetation placement, parked vehicles and road furniture are visual interpretations. Pedestrians are fictional adults with simplified walking and car avoidance; they do not represent real people. The school uses real building outlines with approximate architectural details informed by street references. Traffic uses simplified left-hand routing, spacing and source-positioned signals with authored phases. Ground guardrails use pinned OSM paths; elevated segments are omitted rather than rendered as ground walls. Twelve original kangaroos are placed within a source Red Hill reserve boundary; their positions are fictional. This is not a Street View reconstruction, a current road-condition survey or a driving simulator.

Targets Intel N100 / 8 GB with low rendering settings. Actual target-device performance has not been measured. The playable area covers the inner south and a northern corridor to Civic and eastern public roads to Canberra Airport. The complete lake polygon extends beyond the western drivable boundary. Other Canberra districts remain outside this preview.
